## Research Findings This Week

### JetBrains HAX Study — ICSE 2026 (April 2026)
Mixed-methods study of 800 developers (2 years of telemetry, 62 surveys, follow-up interviews) presented at ICSE 2026 in Rio de Janeiro. Key findings:

- **AI redistributes workflows in ways developers don't perceive.** The headline insight: what developers think AI does to their work and what telemetry shows are consistently misaligned.
- **Productivity: aligned.** AI users typed ~600 more characters/month vs. 75 for non-users. 80%+ reported productivity increases. This one tracks.
- **Editing: major gap.** AI users showed 100 additional deletions/month vs. 7 for non-users — far more curation work than expected. Yet 50% reported no perceived change in editing habits. Developers are spending significantly more time fixing AI output than they realize.
- **Debugging: unchanged.** Debugging sessions remained constant for AI users, despite 50% perceiving code quality improvements. AI is not reducing the debugging burden.
- **Context switching: slightly worse.** AI users showed 6 more IDE activations/month; non-users decreased by 7. Contrary to marketing claims that AI reduces context switching.

Source: [JetBrains Research Blog — ICSE 2026](https://blog.jetbrains.com/research/2026/04/ai-impact-developer-workflows/)

### Faros AI Productivity Paradox Report (April 2026)
Based on 22,000 developers across 2 years of telemetry:

- Teams with high AI adoption complete 21% more tasks and merge 98% more PRs.
- PR review time increases 91% — the human approval bottleneck consumes most of the velocity gain.
- 9% increase in bugs shipped to production with AI usage. Velocity gains come at a quality cost.
- Core thesis: AI-driven coding gains evaporate when review bottlenecks, brittle testing, and slow release pipelines cannot match the new velocity (Amdahl's Law applied to software delivery).

Source: [Faros AI — AI Productivity Paradox](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering)
